## Physio Hot Tip: Knee Pain 🦵

This time of year, I see lots of people with **knee pain** – winter sports injuries are common.

- Quick referrals for acute care and medical assessment alongside physiotherapy improve outcomes.
- Year-round, patients complain of knee pain related to **age, history, and load.**
- Typically, people present with **no major injury**, but painful knees aggravated by:
  - Hills
  - Stairs
  - Sports and exercise
  - Everyday life

**Sound familiar?**

### Common contributing factors:

- Decreased strength
- Biomechanics
- Injury history
- Loading

## Knee Strength Recommendations 💪

The following exercises are **effective, evidence-based, and a great start.**  
I often give these to patients to build endurance and strength in a non-aggravating way.

⚠️ If any cause strong/sharp knee pain or symptoms of concern, **stop and book a consultation.**

- **Normal:** muscle fatigue (0–5/10) that eases with rest between sets.
- **Expected:** DOMS (muscle soreness) after hard work.

---

### 🚴 Cycling

Use increased resistance and interval training to build muscles around the knee.

### ➡️ Straight Leg Raise

1. Straighten knee fully.
2. Lift leg up and **hold to fatigue**.
3. Variations:
   - External hip rotation (leg turned out)
   - Add pulses up and down with knee straight

### 🏋️ Squats / Lunges / Hills / Stairs

There’s no single “perfect squat form” for everyone. **Variation is great!**

Tips:

- Line up kneecap over 2nd toe/midfoot
- Smooth, controlled movement
- Stay turned out at hips to engage stabilisers
- Foot position: weight evenly across little toe border, big toe, and heel
- Reps: to fatigue, but without loss of form

### 🧱 Wall Holds

Build endurance and strength — easy to fit into daily life!

- Stand back against wall, feet away
- Slide down and hold to fatigue

Variations:

- Stance: narrow, wide, turned out
- Increase depth
- Add load (hold weights)
- Increase time

## The Key 🔑

Get started and then build into **individual, sport, and activity-specific strength and conditioning.**

👉 Progressive overload and lifelong strengthening are highly recommended!

## Final Knee Tip of the Month

Movement and biomechanics are **complex and holistic**.  
Knee pain is not just arthritis, tight ITBs, or weak hamstrings.

The **entire lower limb chain** contributes:

- Core
- Pelvic floor
- Pelvis
- Glutes
- Hips
- Knees
- Feet

Plus your **upper body, breathing, nervous system, balance, and beliefs** all play a role in mobility and pain.
